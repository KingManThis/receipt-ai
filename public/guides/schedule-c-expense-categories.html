Schedule C expense categories explained, line by line

If you freelance or run a one-person business in the US, your business expenses go on IRS Schedule C (Form 1040). Here's what belongs on each expense line, with everyday examples.

General information, not tax advice. Rules change; check the current Schedule C instructions or ask a tax professional about your situation.

Lines 12 (depletion), 14 (employee benefit programs), 19 (pension and profit-sharing plans) and 26 (wages) mostly apply to businesses with employees or specialised assets. Home office costs are handled separately (line 30, often with Form 8829).

The 50% meals rule

Business meals, such as taking a client to lunch or eating while travelling for work, are generally only 50% deductible. Keep the receipt and note who you met and the business purpose. ReceiptPilot applies the 50% automatically in your tax summary.

Mixed business and personal use

For a phone, internet plan or car you also use personally, only the business share counts. Keep a simple record of how you worked out the percentage.

Which receipts should I keep?

Keep proof of every expense you deduct: the receipt or invoice showing who you paid, how much, when, and what for. The IRS generally recommends keeping records for at least three years after you file, and longer in some cases. A photo or PDF copy works, as long as it's readable.
