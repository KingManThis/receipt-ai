How to track receipts for taxes as a freelancer

Most freelancers don't lose money because they forget to deduct expenses; they lose it because the receipts are gone by tax time. Here's a simple system that takes about ten minutes a week.

General information, not tax advice.

1. Capture every receipt the same day

Paper receipts fade and disappear. Photograph them the moment you get them, flat and in good light. For emailed receipts, save the PDF. A phone photo is a valid record as long as it's readable.

2. Record the essentials

For each expense you need: who you paid, how much, when, and what it was for. For meals, also note who you met and the business reason.

3. Categorize using Schedule C lines

Use the same categories as your tax form so tax time is just adding up columns. Common ones are Supplies, Office expense, Travel, Meals (50%), Car and truck, and Other expenses for software and subscriptions. See our Schedule C categories guide.

4. Review once a week

Set a weekly reminder. Check new receipts for wrong amounts, missing dates or the wrong category, and remove anything personal.

5. Keep business and personal separate

A separate card or bank account for business makes records far cleaner and is much easier to explain if you're ever asked.

6. Export at tax time

At the end of the year you want three things: a spreadsheet of every expense, totals by category, and copies of the receipts themselves. Hand those to your accountant or enter the totals into your tax software.

How long should I keep receipts?

The IRS generally recommends keeping records for at least three years after you file, and longer in some situations (for example, for assets you depreciate). Digital copies are fine.
